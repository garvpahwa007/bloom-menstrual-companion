# Draft clinician-review note: FOGSI use of PCOD and PCOS in Saheli

**Prepared by Manus AI on 23 October 2026.** This is a focused terminology review, not clinical sign-off for the rest of the app. The copy below is now in the source files; it still needs a qualified clinician’s approval. The Hindi text also needs native medical-language review.

## Current app copy for review

**Lesson title:** “PCOS and the term PCOD.”

**English:** “A FOGSI clinical algorithm booklet expands PCOD as ‘polycystic ovarian disease’ and PCOS as ‘polycystic ovarian syndrome,’ and uses both terms. It does not clearly say whether they mean different diagnoses or alternate names. Saheli uses PCOS as its main clinical term. If a report says PCOD, ask the clinician who issued it what they mean and what was assessed. A label, an app or an ultrasound result alone cannot diagnose you.”

**Hindi draft:** “FOGSI की एक चिकित्सकीय एल्गोरिदम पुस्तिका में PCOD का पूरा नाम ‘polycystic ovarian disease’ और PCOS का ‘polycystic ovarian syndrome’ दिया गया है। पुस्तिका दोनों शब्द इस्तेमाल करती है, लेकिन यह साफ़ नहीं बताती कि वे अलग निदान हैं या एक ही स्थिति के अलग नाम। Saheli मुख्य चिकित्सकीय नाम के रूप में PCOS इस्तेमाल करता है। अगर किसी रिपोर्ट में PCOD लिखा हो, तो रिपोर्ट जारी करने वाले डॉक्टर से पूछें कि उनका क्या मतलब है और किन बातों की जाँच हुई। केवल नाम, ऐप या अल्ट्रासाउंड की रिपोर्ट से निदान नहीं होता।”

The offline Ask question is now “Why do I see both PCOD and PCOS?” / “PCOD और PCOS दोनों शब्द क्यों दिखते हैं?” Its answer gives the same source-based explanation and directs users to ask the clinician who issued a PCOD-labelled report.

## Evidence reviewed and limits

The FOGSI *TOG Algorithms 4* booklet separately expands PCOD as “polycystic ovarian disease” and PCOS as “polycystic ovarian syndrome” in its glossary. Its ovarian-cyst algorithm includes “Can be PCOD,” followed by a separate PCOS section describing a multisystem syndrome.[2] This shows that both labels appear in a FOGSI publication. The wording reviewed does not settle whether the terms are synonyms, distinct diagnoses, or legacy/editorial usage. It also does not establish how commonly patients or clinicians use PCOD in India.

FOGSI’s *Update in Managing PCOS in Women* uses PCOS as its clinical subject and discusses the added care needed when assessing adolescents.[1] WHO’s fact sheet uses PCOS and says diagnosis requires at least two of three features after other causes have been excluded; ovarian morphology is only one feature.[3] This supports keeping the warning that an ultrasound result alone cannot diagnose PCOS. An Indian dermatology review from 2013 describes PCOD historically and says it is now known as PCOS, but that older article does not establish current national consensus or frequency of use.[4] The originally linked FOGSI adolescent FAQ remains blocked and was not used as evidence.[5]

The former app wording, “You may hear PCOD in India,” has been replaced with a narrower statement tied to a specific FOGSI publication. This is an editorial response to the available source, not a claim that terminology has been settled by clinicians.
